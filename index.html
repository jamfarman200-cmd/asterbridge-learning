01 / STUDY STRATEGY

Why rereading isn't the same as remembering

Retrieval practice—trying to recall information without looking—helps reveal what you know and what still needs work.

Read the insight

After reviewing a topic, close your notes and explain it from memory. Write down what you recall, check your gaps and repeat. Short recall sessions spaced over time are often more useful than one long rereading session.

02 / EXAM PREPARATION

Build an exam plan backwards

Start with the test date and break the syllabus into realistic blocks.

Read the insight

List the topics, rank them by difficulty and importance, then schedule practice and review. Reserve time for mixed questions and a final check of recurring errors. A plan should be realistic enough to follow, not impressive enough to abandon.

03 / MATHEMATICS

What to do when a maths problem feels impossible

Stop guessing the next step. Find the first step you don't understand.

Read the insight

Identify the question's givens, write down what you need to find, and recall the relevant rule. If you're stuck, work through a simpler example. Then return to the original problem and explain each step in your own words.

04 / LEARNING HABITS

Turn mistakes into a revision map

Every mistake can reveal a pattern worth practicing.

Read the insight

Keep an error log: the question, what went wrong, the correct reasoning and one similar question to retry later. Review this log before the next test. The goal isn't to collect errors—it's to stop repeating them.

05 / FOCUS & ROUTINE

A focused 25-minute study sprint

Make one session about one clearly defined task.

Read the insight

Choose one goal, remove distractions, work for 25 minutes and take a short break. At the end, note what you completed and what comes next. Adjust the interval if another rhythm works better for you.
